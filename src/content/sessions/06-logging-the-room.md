Week 5 sent the log at a screen. This week it goes back into a physical
space — not a new instrument, the same one, with one new field for a
concrete feature of the environment around an ordinary wait.

## Before the session

Read the [Week 6 lecture](/lectures/week-06/) first. It draws the
distinction this session needs: a room doesn't behave the way a
progress bar does, so this week isn't asking what a space implied about
how much of the wait was left. It's asking whether a concrete feature of
the space relates to how you evaluated the wait (Outcome/notes) at all,
separately from how long the wait felt (Judged duration).

## Finding a genuine entry

Over the week, log one ordinary physical waiting environment you can
safely and accessibly observe — a lobby, a campus space, a transport
stop, a shop or retail queue, or anywhere else you genuinely wait in
person. Use the existing fields from Week 1, and add one new, clearly
labelled field:

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Environment noticed | At least one specific, concrete feature of the physical space you actually noticed during the wait — seating, lighting, noise level, what was (or wasn't) on the walls, temperature, how crowded it was. Not a general verdict like "nice" or "grim" — name the feature. |

Don't rearrange or alter anything about the space to manufacture a more
interesting entry, and don't use a medical facility, emergency service,
or other sensitive setting for this exercise. An ordinary, accessible
wait you were already going to have is enough.

## Comparing evaluation against judged duration

Look at this entry's Judged duration and its Outcome/notes side by side.
Write a short, explicit answer to one question: did the space seem to
affect how you *felt* about the wait, without any sign that it also
changed how *long* the wait felt? A worked example (a model of the
reasoning, not a real entry):

> Context: waiting for a bus at an exposed stop with no shelter and
> nowhere to sit. Clock duration: nine minutes. Judged duration: "about
> ten — roughly accurate." Environment noticed: no seating, no shelter
> from wind, constant traffic noise from the road. Outcome/notes:
> "irritable well before the bus arrived, more than the wait alone
> seemed to justify." **Comparison:** Judged duration tracked clock
> duration closely — nothing here suggests the space changed how long
> the wait felt. Outcome/notes reads worse than the duration alone would
> predict. That gap is the kind of thing Pruyn and Smidts's affect route
> would predict; a single entry can't confirm the environment caused it,
> since tiredness or the specific errand could just as easily explain
> some of it.

## Checking the distraction assumption, if you have a case for it

If any of your existing log entries — from this week or an earlier one —
contain an environmental feature that was intended or assumed to be
distracting (something to watch, something to do, background music or
a screen meant to occupy you), examine whether it appeared to change
your evaluation of that wait. If it didn't, discuss that; it's the same
gap Pruyn and Smidts found with their TV distractor. If your log doesn't
contain a case like this, say so directly. Don't invent or engineer one
just to have something to write about.

## In the session

Bring your entry and your comparison. We compare across the room: whose
Outcome/notes moved independently of Judged duration, who found (or
didn't find) a case for the distraction check, and what's left over that
neither explanation accounts for.

## What this can and can't show

One entry, however specific about the space, can't show that an
environment's attractiveness caused a difference in how you evaluated a
wait — it can only show whether your own experience lines up with, or
departs from, a pattern found in a different real setting entirely. It
also can't tell you that distraction never works; at most it can flag
one case where an assumed-helpful feature didn't behave as expected, if
your log happens to contain one. Don't rearrange a real space or
interfere with a real queue to get a better entry — log what you
actually encountered.

## Afterwards

Keep the log running. Week 7 turns to who else was in that same space
with you — what a queue's own rules make visible, or leave invisible,
about who is served next.

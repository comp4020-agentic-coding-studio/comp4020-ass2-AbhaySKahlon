Your log has an "Expected wait" field you filled in every time since Week
1 and haven't done anything with yet. This session is where that changes.

## Before the session

Read the [Week 3 lecture](/lectures/week-03/) first. It explains why a wait
gets evaluated against what you expected, not just against zero, and why
that's a separate question from whether the wait's judged duration itself
changes. Bring your log.

## Classifying what you already have

Pick at least three existing entries. For each one, compare the "Expected
wait" field against the Clock duration and sort it into one of three
buckets:

- **Shorter than expected** — the wait finished before whatever you'd
  anticipated.
- **Close to expected** — the wait matched what you'd anticipated closely
  enough that you wouldn't call it a surprise either way.
- **Longer than expected** — the wait ran past whatever you'd anticipated.

If an entry has no real "Expected wait" — you genuinely had no expectation
going in — leave it out of this classification rather than inventing one
after the fact.

## Comparing across the classification

Look for a pair of entries with a similar clock duration but different
expectation classifications, and check what's in their Outcome/notes. Ask:
does the entry that ran longer than expected carry a worse outcome or
stronger reaction than the one that was close to or under its expectation,
even though the clock durations are similar? If so, expectation is a
plausible part of the explanation. If the outcomes look similar regardless
of classification, or if your log genuinely can't tell because the entries
differ in other obvious ways too, say that instead.

**A worked comparison** (a model of the reasoning, not a real pair of
entries):

> Entry A: waiting for a coffee order, expected wait "a couple of minutes,"
> clock duration four minutes — longer than expected. Outcome/notes:
> "annoyed, checked my phone twice." Entry B: waiting for a coffee order,
> expected wait "probably five minutes, they looked busy," clock duration
> four minutes — close to expected, arguably shorter. Outcome/notes: "fine,
> didn't think about it." Same clock duration, same kind of wait, different
> expectation classification, different outcome. Expectation looks like a
> plausible part of the story. It isn't the only possible one — A and B
> could also have differed in what occupied their attention, which Week 2
> already showed can move an entry's outcome on its own — so one pair can't
> separate the two candidates, only show that expectation is consistent
> with what changed here.

## Logging one more entry, with the expectation already on record

Add one new entry, in the exact log format from Week 1, from an ordinary
wait where you can genuinely say what you expected before it started —
because you'd been through a similar wait before, or because someone told
you roughly how long it would be. Don't manufacture a mismatch between
what you expect and what happens, and don't manipulate another person, a
queue, or a service to produce one; take the expectation you actually had,
log the actual outcome, and classify it honestly, whichever bucket it lands
in.

## In the session

Bring your classified entries and the new one. We compare across the room:
whose logs show outcomes tracking the expectation classification, whose
don't, and whose logs genuinely can't tell either way because too much
else differs between the entries being compared.

## What this can and can't show

Finding that a longer-than-expected entry also carries a worse outcome is
not proof that missing your expectation *caused* that outcome — attention,
paradigm, and plain bad luck could all be doing some of that work instead,
and a handful of personal entries can't pull them apart. Treat a matching
pattern as a reason to keep the expectation question open, not as something
you've demonstrated.

## Afterwards

Keep the log running. Week 4 asks what you were told *during* the wait,
which is a different question from what you expected walking in — so don't
treat this week's classification as the last word on any entry; you'll be
rereading it again once information is on the table too.

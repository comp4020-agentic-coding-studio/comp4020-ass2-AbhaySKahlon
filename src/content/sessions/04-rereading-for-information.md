Your log's "Available information" field has said "none" more often than
not for three weeks running. This session is where you check what's
different about the entries where it doesn't.

## Before the session

Read the [Week 4 lecture](/lectures/week-04/) first. It draws a specific
line: expectation is what you bring into a wait, information is what you
receive once it's already underway, and this session only works if you
keep those two questions apart.

## Classifying what you already have

Pick at least three existing entries. For each one, look only at what
"Available information" actually contains and sort it into one of two
buckets:

- **Explicit waiting information** — a countdown, a queue position, a
  progress update, an announcement, or a person telling you directly how
  long or how far through the wait you were.
- **No explicit waiting information** — nothing explicitly communicated
  remaining time, position, or progress, even if you happened to guess or
  assume something yourself.

Leave Week 3's expectation classification exactly as it is on these
entries. You're adding a second, independent label, not replacing the
first one.

## Comparing across the classification

Find a pair of entries where the clock duration is similar *and* the
judged duration is similar, but one carries explicit waiting information
and the other doesn't. Look at their Outcome/notes. Write your comparison
in three explicit parts:

1. **Pattern noticed** — what actually differs between the two
   Outcome/notes entries.
2. **Plausible interpretation** — whether the presence or absence of
   explicit waiting information is a reasonable candidate to explain that
   difference.
3. **Alternative explanation or limitation** — at least one other thing
   that could account for the difference instead (different attention,
   different expectation, plain circumstance), or a note that the two
   entries differ in too many other ways to isolate information at all.

**A worked comparison** (a model of the reasoning, not a real pair of
entries):

> Entry A: waiting for a passport ticket to be called, clock duration
> eleven minutes, judged duration "about ten minutes," a screen counting
> down numbers ahead of mine the whole time. Outcome/notes: "fine, watched
> my number get closer." Entry B: waiting at a different counter, clock
> duration twelve minutes, judged duration "about ten minutes," no screen,
> no update, nothing until my name was called. Outcome/notes: "started
> wondering if I'd been skipped, checked the desk twice, annoyed by the
> end." **Pattern noticed:** near-identical clock and judged durations,
> very different Outcome/notes. **Plausible interpretation:** the screen in
> A is a reasonable candidate for the calmer outcome, since it's the clearest
> difference between the two waits. **Alternative explanation/limitation:**
> the two waits were also at different counters on different days, so
> attention, mood beforehand, or something about the specific queue could
> be doing some of that work instead — one pair can't rule that out.

## Logging one more entry, with real information arriving mid-wait

Add one new entry, in the exact log format from Week 1, from an ordinary
wait where explicit waiting information genuinely arrived after the wait
had already started — a countdown that appeared, a queue number update, a
person telling you "about five more minutes" partway through. Don't
arrange for a system or a person to provide this for the sake of the
exercise; log a wait where it happened on its own.

## In the session

Bring your classified entries, your three-part comparison, and the new
entry. We compare across the room: whose logs show Outcome/notes tracking
the information classification once clock and judged duration are held
roughly similar, whose don't, and whose comparisons land honestly in "too
much else differs to tell."

## What this can and can't show

A matching pattern between explicit waiting information and a more
favourable Outcome/notes entry is not proof that the information *caused*
that outcome — your alternative-explanation step exists precisely because
attention, expectation, and circumstance are still live candidates that a
personal log checked against one more field can't rule out. Treat the
pattern as worth investigating further, not as something this session has
demonstrated.

## Afterwards

Keep the log running, but this is the last week that rereads it through a
single new field. Waiting Field Notes, due at the end of this week, asks
for the log itself plus your own interpretation across attention,
expectation, and information together — so before you submit, go back over
all three classifications on your strongest entries at once, rather than
relying on whichever lens each session made you apply in isolation.

The lecture named two gaps the toolkit still had after Week 11: control has
never been classified in your audited system itself, and no claim has ever
been made about how two of its mechanisms relate to each other. This
session closes both, on the same system you've now revisited across Weeks
9, 11, and 12, producing the last working material the Waiting Audit asks
you to submit in full.

## Before the session

Read the [Week 12 lecture](/lectures/week-12/) first. Bring your Week 11
table and your original Queue Case Study.

## Building the tenth row

Look at your audited system's own accessible evidence — published terms,
signage, a stated policy, an observable feature — for anything resembling a
genuine, no-penalty choice to continue or leave. Assign a status: present,
absent, ambiguous, or cannot determine. Then, separately, tier whatever
specific claim you want to make about it.

Your Week 10 experiment can provide additional evidence about a
control-related claim, but it does not by itself establish that the audited
system contains a control mechanism. The audited system must still be
classified from its own accessible evidence. Ask honestly whether your
experiment's context and this system are close enough for the result to
count as relevant evidence here at all — a course-designed simulation or a
different real system is very often not close enough, and saying so is the
correct outcome, not a shortfall in your experiment.

## Building the relationship log

Look back over your ten-mechanism table for pairs where you have an actual,
evidence-grounded reason to suspect a connection — not every pair, and not
a target count. For each one you select:

1. State the specific claim about how the two might relate.
2. Classify it: **interacting** (one mechanism's operation depends on,
   implements, modifies, or is linked to the other), **independent**
   (available evidence supports that they operate separately here), or
   **cannot determine** (available evidence is insufficient to say either
   way).
3. Remember that both mechanisms being present in the same system does not,
   by itself, make them independent — that's a claim you still need
   evidence for, same as interacting.
4. Tier the relationship claim itself — directly evidenced, plausible
   interpretation, or undetermined — separately from the tiers you already
   gave the two mechanisms on their own.
5. State the claim's scope explicitly: what you've found is about how these
   two mechanisms operate in *this* system, not a claim about whether they
   interact in general.

Work through the lecture's café example again if it helps: "the $2 buys the
priority" is interacting, directly evidenced, when the system's own
published terms say so outright — but that finding says nothing about
whether payment and priority are connected at any other café.

## What you produce

**A.** A complete ten-mechanism table — mechanism, status, evidence tier,
justification — with the nine rows from Week 11 unchanged and control added
as the tenth, built fresh from your own system's evidence this week.

**B.** A selective relationship log — mechanism pair, relationship
(interacting / independent / cannot determine), evidence tier,
justification — covering only the pairs you have an evidence-grounded
reason to enter. Short or empty is a legitimate result.

**C.** A short whole-system limitations paragraph: what you could and
couldn't observe, the time window, the impossibility of inferring anyone's
psychology, and the absence of a controlled comparison beyond whatever your
Week 10 experiment actually tested.

This is not a separate submission. A, B, and C are the ten-mechanism
classification, the whole-system synthesis, and the limitations section the
Waiting Audit already asks for — this session produces working drafts of
exactly those three things, not an additional piece of assessed work.

## What's out of bounds

No interviews with staff or customers. No surveys of visitors. No
identifying, naming, or photographing any individual. No intrusive
observation of any kind. No medical, emergency, clinical, or triage
setting. No live intervention on a real, non-consenting person or a real
queue. These are the same rules your Queue Case Study and Week 11 already
worked under — this session doesn't relax or extend them.

## What this can and can't show

A completed table and a short, honest relationship log are not a verdict on
the system — not a redesign, not a recommendation, not a fairness or
efficiency judgement, not a claim about anyone's satisfaction, willingness
to pay, or the organisation's motive. "Cannot determine" and "undetermined"
remain correct, complete answers wherever your evidence actually stops
there, for a mechanism, for control specifically, or for a relationship
between two mechanisms — none of them are weaker results than a busier
table with more boxes filled in.

## Afterwards

The Waiting Audit, due at the end of this week, asks for exactly what
you've built: all ten mechanisms, the tenth classified from this system's
own evidence rather than inherited from your experiment, a whole-system
synthesis drawn from whatever relationships your evidence actually
supports, and a limitations section stated plainly. Nothing here has been
building toward proving the course's thesis — it's been building toward
being able to say, honestly and specifically, what you can and can't
actually say about one real system, after twelve weeks of learning how to
tell the difference.

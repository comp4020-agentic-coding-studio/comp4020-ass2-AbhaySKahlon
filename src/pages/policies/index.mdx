# Policies and support

Assessment briefs link back to these rules rather than restating them; if a
brief and this page ever disagree, this page is the one that's current.

## Assessment and extensions

Each assessment is due at the time stated on its page. Ask for an extension by
email before the deadline, naming the new date you need; extensions requested
after a deadline has passed need a documented medical or compassionate reason.
Work submitted late without an agreed extension loses 5% of its available mark
per day, including weekends, until it reaches zero.

## Accessibility

If a course requirement — a live session, a due date, a fieldwork component —
conflicts with an access need, tell the convenor as early as you can, ideally
before Week 4. Alternative formats and adjusted arrangements are agreed
individually and in confidence; there's no separate form to fill in first.

## AI assistance

You may use AI tools to help build, code or edit this site, and to talk
through an idea before you write it up — declare that assistance briefly in
the relevant submission, the same way you'd declare any other help. What you
may not do is let a tool invent the substance of your work: a wait you didn't
observe, a source you haven't read, or a number you can't point to in your own
notes. An assignment that reports something you didn't actually see or find is
a misconduct matter regardless of how it was produced.

## Academic integrity

The Week 1 wait-observation log is a personal record — expect to compare notes
with classmates, but write your own interpretation of your own observations.
Every claim drawn from Block & Zakay, Block, Hancock & Zakay, Hui & Tse, Pruyn
& Smidts or Maister must be attributed to that source, not presented as your
own finding. Submitting someone else's account of a wait as your own
observation is treated the same as any other plagiarism.

## Experiment ethics

Week 10's Waiting Experiment and Week 12's Waiting Audit both involve studying
a real wait, so both are bound by the same constraint:

- default to self-observation, a simulation, or a controlled
  interface/prototype;
- only run a live intervention on a real waiting system when it is genuinely
  low-risk, non-disruptive, privacy-preserving, and authorised wherever
  authorisation is needed — never disrupt an actual clinic, emergency-service
  or similarly essential queue to test an idea;
- name your own confounds and limitations in the write-up;
- never present a small observational or intervention exercise as strong
  causal proof — describe what it does and doesn't show.

The Waiting Experiment and Waiting Audit briefs both point back to this
policy rather than restating it.
